Tracking Pipeline

NEON BREACH tracks three body parts in VR: the head (centre eye) and the left and right hands. Each hand is tracked either as a controller or as an articulated hand skeleton. The headset's tracking system estimates poses in its own tracking space. OpenXR reports them relative to the application's reference space, Unity converts them into its coordinate system, and the game maps them into the virtual world ([[f-track-pipe]]).

Coordinate Systems and Handedness

OpenXR uses a right-handed coordinate system with +X right, +Y up and −Z forward. Unity uses a left-handed system with +X right, +Y up and +Z forward. Converting between them mirrors the Z axis. For positions this negates z; for rotation quaternions, mirroring one axis negates the quaternion components of the two other axes ([[l-xrvec]]):

pU = (x, y, −z)      qU = (−qx, −qy, qz, qw)(6.1) The mapping is its own inverse, so the same formula converts Unity to OpenXR. Unity's OpenXR plug-in applies it in the XrVector3f and XrQuaternionf constructors.

This convention mattered in practice. During verification, a 15° downward head pitch was first converted by hand with Eq. 6.1 before it was passed to the Mock Runtime's raw SetSpace call. Unity reported it back as 345°, a 15° upward pitch, which showed that the call already performs the conversion, so it had been applied twice. Passing the Unity-space rotation directly gave exactly 15.0°. The conformance-automation API also converts internally, and the right-hand pose set through it was read back unchanged ([[s-mockvr]]).

Tracking origin modes

OpenXR offers a LOCAL space (origin at the initial head pose; Unity's Device mode) and a STAGE space (origin on the floor at the centre of the play area; Unity's Floor mode). With Floor origin a standing user's head is reported at about 1.6–1.8 m; with Device origin it is near 0. The rig handles both with the height calibration in Section 6.4. During verification the input subsystem reported origin mode Floor.

Transform Hierarchy and the Registration Chain

Registration means placing tracked objects at the correct place in the virtual world. NEON BREACH registers the tracking space to the world through a fixed chain of transforms ([[f-hier]]):

xworld = TPlayer(p, ψ) · TCameraPivot(o) · ( xtrack + h ŷ )(6.2) TPlayer: the player's world position p and yaw ψ (moved by locomotion, snap turns and the CharacterController); TCameraPivot: the tracking-space origin o inside the player (shifted by room-scale compensation); xtrack: the device pose reported by OpenXR; h: the calibrated height offset; ŷ: the up vector.

The chain was checked with known inputs during the Mock Runtime session. The Player object stood at (0, 0.05, 0) (its saved position, [[f-insp-player]]), the head was reported at the tracking-space origin, and the first calibration therefore chose h = 1.6 m. [[t-registration]] compares the values predicted by Equation 6.2 with the values the game produced.

Height Calibration and Recentering

A tracking-space height of zero means different things on different runtimes, and seated players need their view lifted to standing height so that cover and enemies line up. On the first valid head sample after start-up, after a recenter, or after the Seated option changes, VRRig.Track computes the offset h:

h = max(0, 1.62 − yhead)  (seated)      h = 1.6 if yhead < 0.7 m, otherwise 0  (standing)(6.3) The standing rule detects device-relative tracking (head near zero) and lifts it to a typical eye height; a floor-relative headset is left unchanged. Recenter clears the calibration and re-centres the player under the head.

Room-Scale Head Following

When the player physically walks, the head moves away from the body collider. If nothing corrected this, enemies would aim at the wrong place and walls would not stop the player. FollowHead ([[l-follow]]) moves the CharacterController towards the head's horizontal position through CharacterController.Move, which respects walls, and shifts the camera pivot back by the distance actually moved, so that the camera does not jump.

Δ = projXZ(pcamera − pbody),   m = CC.Move(Δ),   o ← o − projXZ(m)(6.4) If |Δ| < 2 cm nothing happens, which filters out head sway. If a wall blocks the move, m is shorter than Δ, and the body stays at the wall.

Turning about the Head

A snap or smooth turn rotates the player object about its own origin. In room-scale play the head is usually not above that origin, so rotating the body would swing the view sideways. That is disorienting and a known sickness trigger. The rig measures the head's horizontal displacement caused by the rotation and cancels it by moving the pivot ([[l-turn]]), so the rotation happens about the head:

d = projXZ( Ry(Δψ) r − r ),    o ← o − d(6.5) r: the vector from the body origin to the head; Δψ: the turn angle (45° by default for snap turns). The Mock Runtime test showed a single 45° step (0° → 315°).

Hand Pose and Gesture Geometry

For controller-free play, HandTracking derives an aim pose and two gestures from the joint positions ([[l-hands]]). [[t-gesture]] lists the thresholds. The pinch has two thresholds (hysteresis), so a pinch is a small state machine: it starts below 2.0 cm and ends only above 3.5 cm. Fluctuations between the two thresholds therefore cannot produce repeated clicks.

Mixed Reality: Spatial Understanding of the Room

In MR mode the virtual enemies must move inside the user's real room. The game builds its spatial model of the room from the play-area boundary that the user drew during headset set-up. RoomPolygon queries every running XRInputSubsystem for TryGetBoundaryPoints. If a polygon with at least three points is returned, it is used (UsedRealBoundary = true); otherwise a 3.5 × 3.5 m square is used as a fallback. BuildRoom ([[l-buildroom]]) then:

  1. computes the centroid c of the polygon;
  2. adds an invisible floor collider (40 × 1 × 40 m, top at y = 0) on the Environment layer;
  3. for every edge from a to b of length L ≥ 5 cm, adds an invisible wall collider (0.3 × 3 × (L + 0.3) m) at the edge midpoint, pushed 0.15 m outward along normalise(mid − c) and oriented along the edge;
  4. places N = 12 spawn points at equal arc-length intervals dk = P·k/N around the perimeter P, each pulled towards the centroid by min(0.5 m, 0.4·|c − p|);
  5. marks the NavMesh as stale, so that ArenaManager rebuilds it from the new colliders and enemies plan paths inside the real room.

Measured outcome. In the Mock Runtime session the MR mode produced RoomSize = (3.50, 3.50), 4 wall colliders, 12 spawn points and a rebuilt NavMesh, and the first enemy spawned 2.5 m from the player inside the room ([[f-vr-mr]]). The Mock Runtime provides no boundary, so the fallback path was exercised. The real-boundary path uses the same code with the polygon from the headset.

Limitation. The boundary describes the free floor area, not furniture or wall geometry. Scene understanding (Meta's scene model, planes and meshes) would allow enemies to appear behind real furniture; this is listed as future work.

Collision Detection and Physics Layers

Every body that moves under player or AI control uses a Unity CharacterController capsule (the player: radius 0.35 m, height 1.8 m; enemies: per type, e.g. a grunt has radius 0.4 m and height 1.5 m), whose move-and-slide resolves collisions against static geometry. Gameplay queries use explicit raycasts with layer masks. [[t-layers]] lists the layers and [[l-layers]] the runtime collision matrix.
